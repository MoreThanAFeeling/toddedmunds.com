An invention for my brother

My brother Fritz has used a wheelchair for more than 30 years. When I redesigned my basement as a place to spend time with friends, I wanted him to be able to come down too. So I designed a way to get him there.

The result is the Stairwell Elevator: a patented residential elevator that sits hidden under an ordinary staircase. When it's needed, the stairs move out of the way and the platform lifts. I unveiled the prototype at the Midwest Ability Summit in Overland Park, and it's now offered to homeowners who want to stay in the homes they love.

Visit StairwellElevator.com Watch the FOX4 story

Why it's different

  • No elevator shaft, pit or overhead clearance needed
  • Uses the space under the stairs, not closets or living space
  • Nearly invisible when lowered, so the home keeps its look
  • Fits most homes with stairs, for aging in place and accessibility

About

My career has followed the factory's journey from control systems to AI. I started out building packaging machine control systems, became a solution architect at Rockwell Automation, built an IoT business at a systems integrator, and helped build Cisco's industrial automation business.

At Dell Technologies I built the smart manufacturing business to more than $2B in annual revenue. Today I set strategy for manufacturing infrastructure and physical AI, working with the world's leading manufacturers and alliance partners.

Speaking

I'm a frequent speaker at Hannover Messe and NVIDIA GTC. Topics include physical AI and the AI-native factory, digital twins from simulation to decision, edge computing as the foundation for industrial AI, private 5G for operations, and building the business case for manufacturing AI at scale.

I also chair the Digital Twin Consortium Manufacturing Workgroup. I hold a BS in Electrical and Computer Engineering from Kansas State University and two US patents.
